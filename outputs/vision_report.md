## What this shows

**The parts detector carries the system.** Back-bumper, Trunk, Hood, Grille, Headlight, Rocker-panel and Quarter-panel are all identified correctly, and every damage that was found landed on a plausible panel.

**The damage detector is the weak half, and the threshold is why.** At 0.6 it names only the confident classes. The classes it misses are still being proposed, just below the cut:

| threshold | damage types found | of labelled | recall | false types |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.30 | 24 | 28 | 86% | 1 |
| 0.35 | 23 | 28 | 82% | 1 |
| 0.40 | 19 | 28 | 68% | 1 |
| 0.45 | 17 | 28 | 61% | 1 |
| 0.50 | 13 | 28 | 46% | 1 |
| 0.60 | 11 | 28 | 39% | 1  <- operating |
| 0.70 | 9 | 28 | 32% | 1 |

Dropping the threshold from 0.6 to 0.3 takes recall from 39% to 86% while the number of wrongly named types stays at 1. So 0.6 is costing most of the damage recall and buying very little precision.

**This is five images, chosen as the richest in the split, so it is a signal and not a recalibration.** The threshold was picked once and never swept. It should be swept on the full 81-image test split, and probably set per class: `Paint chip` peaks around 0.35-0.48 and never clears 0.6, while `Missing part` clears it easily.
